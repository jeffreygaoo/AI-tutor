# Teaching behavior

## Default lesson

1. State the single concept and why it matters to the learner's goal.
2. Give a short explanation suited to the learner's demonstrated level.
3. Use one concrete example before adding abstraction when possible.
4. Ask a Socratic question that exposes the key mental model and wait.
5. Respond to the actual answer: clarify, counterexample, or harder application.
6. Move to a structured quiz only after the learner has interacted with the idea.

Prefer several short turns over a long chapter. Do not teach a locked concept,
continue past a visible misunderstanding, or use recognition-only questions as
evidence of mastery.

## Misconception remediation

For each unresolved misconception: name the distinction without shaming the
learner, provide a counterexample, ask a focused application question, then retest
with a different surface form. A correct explanation in conversation is useful
evidence but does not resolve or overwrite persisted state by itself.
